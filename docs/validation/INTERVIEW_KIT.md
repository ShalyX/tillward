# Tillward business and payer interviews

Prepared October 31 for Wave 2. This is a research kit, not customer evidence. No interview has been completed by creating this file. Keep identifiable notes outside the public repository; publish anonymized summaries or quotes only with consent.

## Who to speak with

Our proposed research target, not a Mezo rule: 5-10 independent agencies, studios or sellers who hold BTC and have actual operating expenses. Record whether they already borrow against BTC, use stablecoins, and can receive MUSD. Include people who reject the premise. Ask at least three businesses to introduce a payer or let us examine a real payment process without collecting private customer data. BTC ownership alone is not qualification for borrowing.

## Twenty-minute conversation

1. Tell me about your last customer payment and the expenses you needed to cover before it arrived. How did you bridge that gap? Ask for the actual sequence and rough ranges, not wallet balances or documents.
2. Have you used BTC-backed borrowing? What made you use or avoid it? How do liquidation and redemption affect that decision?
3. Which assets do customers pay you in today? Who chooses the asset and network? Who converts it for expenses, at what cost and with what delay?
4. Have you received MUSD specifically? If not, what would have to change before you accepted it? Do not treat familiarity with USDC as MUSD acceptance.
5. Would an actual customer agree to pay an invoice in MUSD on Mezo? What funding, wallet, accounting, or off-ramp step would stop them? Ask to speak with a payer; the seller's guess is weaker evidence.
6. Show the Tillward testnet flow, labeled as test assets. Ask the person to explain the receipt split in their own words. Can they distinguish available cash, cash floor, repayment allocation and minimum-debt closure?
7. How would you choose a receipt percentage and cash floor? What would make you change them? How would you respond to a falling BTC collateral ratio? Never imply repayment removes collateral risk.
8. Which part, if any, saves work compared with native borrowing plus manual repayment? What would make the product unusable?
9. Who would pay for this service and from which budget? First ask without suggesting a price. Then compare a flat monthly plan with a disclosed routing fee. Record objection, preferred model and an actual willingness-to-pay range, not a forced yes.
10. Would you take a concrete next step: a second session with a payer or a supervised testnet pilot? Record agreed follow-up separately from compliments or hypothetical intent. Do not request real funds.

## Evidence record per interview

Use an anonymized ID, interview date, business type, qualification (BTC/borrowing/payment assets), current workaround, seller acceptance of MUSD, payer acceptance (observed / seller-reported / unknown), acquisition/off-ramp obstacle, split/floor comprehension, pricing response, strongest objection, and concrete next step. For every conclusion, label it observed, self-reported, hypothetical or unknown.

Ask permission before recording or quoting. Avoid private keys, balances, customer identities and confidential invoices. Store raw notes in the private workspace; use docs/validation/results.csv only for anonymized evidence approved for publication.

## Optional learning checkpoint

Continue the pilot if at least two businesses commit to a concrete testnet session and at least one actual payer confirms a feasible MUSD funding/payment route. This is an optional internal learning checkpoint, not a Mezo entry requirement or statistical proof of demand. The owner has asked to hold interview work for now. If interest exists but MUSD acceptance blocks payment, record that dependency and assess distribution options before adding connectors. If the working-capital need or receipt-linked repayment value is absent, return to product review with findings; do not quietly broaden the target user or invent traction.
