Tillward — BTC working capital. Track 1: DeFi. New project built for this buildathon, originally named Paydown. Working Mezo testnet deployment (chain 31611).

Tillward gives BTC-holding businesses MUSD working capital and repays the native Mezo loan from the owner's chosen share of customer receipts, while preserving an operating cash floor.

Workflow: create business account → supply BTC and borrow MUSD → set repayment share/cash floor → create invoice → customer approves the exact MUSD amount and pays → eligible receipt allocations reduce that account's native debt. Owner controls include history, pause/edit future routing, MUSD top-up, loan closure and released BTC recovery. MUSD is the loan, checkout and repayment asset; no MEZO-token integration claimed.

Proof: a separate wallet paid 500 MUSD and 100 reduced native debt. Later recorded totals reached 502 collected / 100.4 repaid. Rabby-in-Chrome QA completed a 2,000 MUSD loan / 0.045 test BTC, payment, repayment, top-up, closure and return of collateral. The hosted keeper enrolled three accounts and preserved a repayment across restart. All evidence uses test assets, not customer revenue/adoption. No mainnet deployment or independent audit.

Target: independent agencies/studios/sellers holding BTC, familiar with collateralized borrowing and willing to collect MUSD. Desktop EVM-wallet flow, responsive read/checkout; no regional adoption or mobile-wallet claim.

Stack: Solidity 0.8.24, Foundry, TypeScript, Vite, viem, native Mezo MUSD/BorrowerOperations/HintHelpers, Node.js keeper/systemd, Vercel, GitHub Actions.

Next: within 2 weeks, interview 5–10 businesses and validate MUSD acceptance and invoice/repayment needs; within 4 weeks, a small testnet pilot, broader wallet/RPC failure coverage and operational restore/monitoring work; within 8 weeks, independent review if demand supports continuing and a separate mainnet-readiness decision.
